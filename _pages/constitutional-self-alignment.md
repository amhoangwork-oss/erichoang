*Work in progress*

I'm investigating whether a language model can improve its own alignment through self-critique and on-policy distillation, with minimal supervised or RLHF intervention. This project is still in its infancy.

## The question

Can a model use explicit principles to critique its own responses and learn from that critique in a way that changes how it actually behaves? I'm interested in whether the improvement carries over to unfamiliar situations, including cases where saying the right things about its principles is easier than following them.

## Why I'm working on it

My earlier work looked at how models could be poisoned and how attacks could be detected. I now want to investigate how models could help maintain their own alignment as they become more capable. I take recursive self-improvement seriously, and I want to understand what would let us trust the systems involved in developing their successors.

For me, that means evaluating behavior against a stated reference and looking for situations the evaluation misses. A better score or a more convincing self-critique would leave me asking whether the model's behavior has actually changed, especially when it can plan and use tools.
